The challenge

The bank had 20 million customers. Its messages treated them all the same.

Post-COVID, SME lending was falling. Communication was product-led and rules-based: the same offers, sent the same way, regardless of what a business actually needed. The bank wanted to anticipate needs and reach out at the right moment, but its analytics were on-premise and slow, and nothing connected insight to the customer experience.

My role

I turned data into experiences customers would act on.

I partnered with data science to turn their outputs into behaviour-led segments, customer journeys and messages. My contribution was defining the customer need, designing the next action and making each journey measurable.

Who I led and influenced

  • Partnered · data scienceShaped how model outputs were translated into customer needs, segments and journeys.
  • Influenced · marketing and product leadersMade the case for needs-based journeys over product pushes.
  • Led · 3 client-side designersSet direction, reviewed their work and shaped what each one owned.

Decisions I owned

  • Segments from behaviour, not demographics. Groups were defined by what businesses did and needed, so journeys matched real moments.
  • One message, one moment. Each journey answered a single need at the time it mattered, rather than bundling offers.
  • Design within the bank’s brand. The work focused on message, structure and journey, inside an established brand system.
Research to segments

Research and data showed us who we were really designing for.

We ran discovery workshops with marketing, product and analytics, then interviewed SME owners about how they managed money and when they needed credit. Combined with the bank’s behavioural data, this gave us segments the whole team could design and target around.

Design and data

The model predicted the need. Design made it worth acting on.

Data science combined behavioural signals to anticipate customer needs. My work began where the prediction ended: translating an output into a relevant segment, a clear journey, a useful message and a measurable next action.

Goal 1Predict customer needs

Over the next 3 to 12 months, so we could reach businesses early, such as those about to need a loan or a payments solution.

Goal 2Predict long-term value

To spot customers whose needs and value were about to grow, or who were at risk of leaving.

The solution

Designing for one business need at a time.

For each behaviour-led segment, I designed the moment, the message and the next step across email, in-app prompts and landing pages. Personalisation meant making the journey more relevant, not exposing sensitive information or making customers feel watched.

Hyper-personalisation

Three audiences, three ways of speaking to them.

The 360° view told us who was likely to need lending and when; the research told us why they hesitated. Together they defined three audiences, each with its own message, channel and next step.

Four principles behind the journey design

Segment by behaviour, not demographics.

Use signals of business need to guide the experience rather than broad customer categories.

Design for one need at one moment.

Fewer, more relevant messages were designed to make the bank feel more helpful and less pushy.

Connect every message to an action.

Make the next step clear, whether it is an application, a comparison or a conversation with an adviser.

Test the journey, not only the interface.

Every journey was designed with a test plan, giving the team a repeatable way to improve after we left.

What I’d do differently

  • Instrument design changes from day one, so each journey’s own impact is measured separately from the wider programme.
  • Bring SME owners back in after launch, not only at the start.
