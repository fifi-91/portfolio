A design leadership decision

One shared experience, without three identical products.

The three streams needed different journeys: Discovery prioritised exploration, Finance required considered decisions, and Manage supported ongoing resident needs. Allowing each stream to define its own navigation and component patterns would have fragmented the experience.

I chose a shared information architecture and bilingual component foundation, while keeping stream-specific interactions where the user need justified them. I used cross-stream design and engineering reviews to resolve dependencies before implementation.

The trade-off was more alignment and negotiation up front, rather than faster independent decisions within each stream. The result was a coherent foundation that could support distinct journeys without duplicating the whole system.

03The system

From research to a system engineering could build.

Behavioural research reshaped the IA from search → buy to discover → finance → manage → community. The design system enforced parity across LTR and RTL from atoms up. Illustrations below recreate each stage without client material.

Homeownership is a relationship, not a transaction.

Behavioural research surfaced the real friction: not interest, but trust. Maintenance was the most-needed, least-trusted service category. Buyers wanted clearer pricing and transparent communication before, during and after.

  • Qual + quant studies with prospective buyers and existing residents
  • Service-quality benchmarking across the regional category
  • Findings clustered into themes that set the design priorities
