03The system

One structure, permissions decide the view.

Rather than a separate experience for each user type, the platform has one navigation. Who you are decides what sits inside it: a prospective agency sees a route to becoming a partner, a certified partner sees how to grow the partnership, and an admin also manages the team. Each section answers one question the user brings with them.

Interactive: switch user type to see the same structure change its contents. Highlighted items are specific to that role. Labels are generalised from the real platform.

03The decision

Build the foundation before the AI.

AI features were part of the ambition from the start: an assistant, AI summaries and personalised guidance. While they were still being scoped, I recommended taking them out of the MVP.

What was wanted
The business wanted AI to set the platform apart at launch. Engineering needed stable foundations first: sign-on, access, content and accounts.
What I chose
Launch the core journeys first, and bring AI in once its scope and data were ready.
What it cost
A less striking launch story.
What it produced
A foundation the AI features can rely on, instead of features built on ground that was still moving. [How you brought stakeholders round.]
